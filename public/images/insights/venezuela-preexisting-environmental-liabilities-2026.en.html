Pre-existing environmental liabilities in Venezuela
What does the reactivating operator inherit? The law draws no distinction between the damage you caused and the damage you found.
María Eugenia Reyes · Ágora Abogados SC · 2026
The core rule
Whoever operates, answers. Environmental liability is strict: no proof of fault is required. The current operator that maintains or fails to remediate a damaged condition is liable, even if the damage was caused by a prior operator.
Due diligence — 5 fronts before closing
01
Authorization history
File at MINEC: prior operator's permits, sanctions, open proceedings. These do not expire with a change of operator.
02
Soil and groundwater condition
Site assessment to detect contamination — the basic technical tool to quantify the liability and size the remediation obligation.
03
Hazardous waste inventory
Under Decree 2,635 and the Hazardous Substances Law: identified, classified, and disposed of as required.
04
Water Law compliance
Water use concessions and discharge permits. Historical operation without authorizations creates regulatory liability activatable against the new operator.
05
Community liabilities
Affected communities may demand remediation. Conflicts from the prior operator may be reactivated when the new one arrives.
Contractual tools — 5 protection mechanisms
01
Specific reps and warranties
The seller declares the environmental condition of the asset precisely. A generic compliance statement is not enough.
02
Dedicated indemnities
For liabilities identified in due diligence: liability caps, coverage periods, and notice and defense mechanisms.
03
Price retention (escrow/holdback)
A portion of the price held until remediation is certified or the administrative proceeding is closed.
04
Condition precedent or price adjustment
If liabilities are uncertain, remediation may be a closing condition or the price may be adjusted to reflect estimated management costs.
05
Environmental reps for lenders
Lenders require environmental representations as a disbursement condition. An unmanaged liability may trigger loan default events.
