The ESIA is a legality condition for reactivating strategic assets in Venezuela

Decree No. 1.257 requires environmental assessment across the project life cycle: commencement, reactivation, and closure.

Core rule
No covered activity may begin, restart, or cease regularly without an approved Environmental and Sociocultural Impact Assessment.
1

Commencement

Approval must exist before operations start. Treating the ESIA as a post-closing formality creates administrative and criminal exposure.

2

Reactivation

Restarting idle wells, refineries, plants, or power facilities may require a new or updated assessment as conditions and permits change.

3

Closure

Suspension and abandonment plans are supervised to prevent spills, hazardous waste, infrastructure deterioration, and soil or water contamination.

Compliance risk

Non-compliance can trigger fines, suspension orders, restoration measures, permit revocations, and civil liability.

Officer exposure

Directors, managers, and legal representatives may face personal liability when they authorize, consent to, or fail to supervise violations.
