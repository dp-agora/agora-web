*SENIAT has published a new general invoicing regime that eliminates the lists of activities required to use fiscal printers, turns pre-printed invoice books into a contingency mechanism, eliminates free forms, and lets taxpayers choose between two means of issuance: digital/electronic means or fiscal printers. It will enter into force on April 19, 2027.*

For 15 years, the means of invoicing was not necessarily optional: it was chosen by article 8 of SENIAT Administrative Ruling No. SNAT/2011/0071 (Official Gazette No. 39,795 of November 8, 2011) ("Ruling 71"). Anyone who sold food, repaired vehicles, ran a hair salon or operated a parking lot had to invoice through a fiscal printer. Restaurants, bakeries and bars were bound even if they did not reach the applicable revenue threshold. All other taxpayers could choose between free forms (*formas libres*) and pre-printed invoice books (*formatos*). Mass-service providers had been able to issue their invoices digitally since before 2011. In late 2024 this regime was supplemented by SENIAT Administrative Ruling No. SNAT/2024/000102 (Official Gazette No. 43,032 of December 19, 2024) ("Ruling 102"), which enabled digital invoicing as an optional regime subject to prior authorization, and closed it precisely to those required to use fiscal printers exclusively.

The current regime remains in force until April 18, 2027. SENIAT Administrative Ruling No. SNAT/2026/00093 (Official Gazette No. 43,484 of October 21, 2026) ("Ruling 93") repeals, among others, Ruling 71, Ruling 102 and SENIAT Administrative Ruling No. SNAT/2018/00141 (Official Gazette No. 41,518 of November 6, 2018) ("Ruling 141"), which governed authorized printshops, and establishes a different regime built on a simple premise: the taxpayer decides how to invoice, and SENIAT focuses on receiving the information. The regime applies to the issuance of invoices, delivery orders or shipping guides, debit and credit notes, VAT and income tax withholding certificates, and certificates of exonerated output VAT.

## The core change: free choice of the means of issuance

- Article 5 of Ruling 93 frames the whole system. The adoption of any of the authorized means *"is left to the free choice of the taxpayer."* There are no activity lists; the only sector-specific exception is explained below.
- Article 8 of Ruling 71 disappears, and with it its 21 subsections listing the activities required to invoice through fiscal printers. The criterion of gross revenues above 1,500 tax units also disappears.
- The second recital states that Ruling 93 seeks to enshrine the free choice of the invoicing means *"by eliminating categorical restrictions by economic sector."*
- A single sector-specific restriction survives. Producers and distributors of alcohol and alcoholic beverages may only invoice by digital means.

## The two authorized means of issuing invoices

Article 4 narrows the catalogue to two:

1. **Digital or electronic means.** These include two channels: (i) SENIAT's Web Portal and Mobile App, which are free institutional channels available to everyone; and (ii) the digital printer (*imprenta digital*) or any other authorized technology provider, whose certification is still governed by SENIAT Administrative Ruling No. SNAT/2024/000121 (Official Gazette No. 43,032 of December 19, 2024) ("Ruling 121"), which remains in force.
2. **Fiscal printers.** Equipment previously authorized by SENIAT, with built-in fiscal and audit memory. For the invoice to give rise to an input VAT credit, the printer must print the purchaser's name or corporate name on the invoice itself.

The "free forms" produced by authorized printshops, which Ruling 71 recognized as an ordinary means, are removed from the catalogue.

## Pre-printed invoice books become a contingency tool

- Article 6 of Ruling 93 reserves forms produced by printshops *"solely and exclusively as a contingency mechanism"* for eight closed cases: (i) prolonged power outages, (ii) severe internet disruption, (iii) proven failure or breakdown of the invoicing means, (iv) unscheduled critical maintenance of the central digital system, (v) the fiscal printer's inability to issue notes and guides, (vi) transactions on behalf of third parties, (vii) export transactions, and (viii) transactions requiring more than one copy of the invoice. Ruling 93 does not say so, but based on past experience (e.g., the 2019 blackouts and the COVID pandemic), the burden of proving the contingency will fall on the taxpayer (see Political-Administrative Chamber of the Supreme Tribunal of Justice, decision No. 00276 of April 25, 2025, case: Samsung Electronics de Venezuela, C.A. v. Treasury). It is therefore advisable to substantiate the contingency with every available means of evidence that is contemporaneous with or immediately follows the emergency (e.g., screenshots, a letter or e-mail notifying SENIAT of the contingency, an extra litem judicial or notarial inspection, etc.).
- The Sole Paragraph imposes a permanent obligation: every taxpayer must keep duly authorized contingency forms at its place of business. This also applies to those who invoice 100% digitally.
- Article 7 creates a regularization obligation that did not exist before. Once the contingency is over, the issuer must record all documents issued on paper and send that information on the following day, in PDF format, to the authorized digital printer or to SENIAT's application programming interface (API), depending on its main means of issuance.

## Who the new regime applies to

Article 2 of Ruling 93 identifies the obligated parties in three items, compared with four in Ruling 71. The wording is shorter, but the scope is broader, because the test for coverage changes: it no longer looks at the issuer's revenue level, but at the function of the document issued.

1. **Legal entities and economic entities without legal personality.** The wording is identical to that of Ruling 71. It covers consortia, communities and other entities operating without their own legal personality.
2. **Individuals.** This is where the change lies. Ruling 71 divided individuals into three categories, depending on whether their annual revenues exceeded 1,500 tax units and whether they were ordinary VAT taxpayers. Ruling 93 eliminates that gradation: individuals are covered, whether or not they are liable for VAT or income tax, when they issue tax documents that must be used as evidence of an expense or of input VAT.

   The test is no longer who issues the document, but what the document is for. The issuer's VAT or income tax status becomes irrelevant. What matters is that the document will support a deductible expense or a tax credit for the recipient. In practice, every individual who invoices a company falls within the regime, regardless of revenue level.
3. **Formal VAT taxpayers.** For the first time, they appear expressly in the general invoicing rules: taxpayers that carry out exclusively VAT-exempt or exonerated activities. They were governed by SENIAT Administrative Ruling No. SNAT/2003/1.677 (Official Gazette No. 37,677 of April 25, 2003) ("Ruling 1,677"). Under Ruling 93, their invoices must also carry the phrase *"Contribuyentes Formales del IVA"* (Formal VAT Taxpayers) for those transactions.

   The scope of this inclusion should be clarified, because it lends itself to confusion. Ruling 93 repeals Ruling 1,677 only *"as regards the invoicing rules"* (First Repealing Provision), with the same partial scope it gives to SENIAT Administrative Rulings No. SNAT/2009/0102 (Official Gazette No. 39,290 of October 22, 2009) (income tax withholding on lottery prizes and control rules for lottery operators) and SNAT/2013/0078 (Official Gazette No. 40,318 of December 18, 2013) (air passenger transport). Articles 3 and 4 of Ruling 1,677, which set the requirements for documents supporting sales and allowed some of them to be waived, are therefore repealed. The other obligations of formal VAT taxpayers under Ruling 1,677 remain: registration in the Unique Tax Information Registry ("RIF"), purchase and sales ledgers, their filing, and the informative return. The invoicing regime of formal VAT taxpayers changes, but not their other formal duties.

## Excluded transactions

Article 3 places the following transactions outside the scope of Ruling 93:

1. Sales of real estate.
2. Non-definitive imports of movable goods.
3. Banking and financial transactions governed by the Banking Sector Institutions Law, with one express exception: financial leasing is subject to Ruling 93.
4. Transactions carried out on stock exchanges and agricultural exchanges.
5. Services rendered under an employment relationship.
6. Domestic public land passenger transport services.
7. Medical, healthcare and educational services, and services rendered by parks or museums, when provided directly by public entities or by non-profit institutions exempt from income tax.
8. Common condominium expenses, itemized in the receipt provided for in the Horizontal Property Law.
9. Activities and transactions carried out by entities created by the National Executive under the Organic Tax Code, and by the States and Municipalities, to ensure the efficient administration of the taxes within their competence.
10. Lodging, food and related services provided to students, elderly persons, persons with disabilities, persons with special needs or sick persons, when rendered within an institution devoted exclusively to serving such users, provided it is exempt from income tax.

## Invoice requirements: what changes and what does not

Article 8 consolidates into a single catalogue the requirements that Ruling 71 spread across several articles, depending on the means of issuance and the issuer's status. That consolidation, rather than the creation of new requirements, is the real change. The two levels should be distinguished:

- **Time of issuance: now general.** Ruling 71 required it only for invoices issued through fiscal printers, without prescribing a format. Article 8(5) extends it to all means and sets the HH.MM.SS format, indicating whether the invoice was issued before or after noon.
- **Control number for digital means.** Consecutive and unique for each issuer, with a two-digit identifier and a sequence of up to eight digits. When requested for the first time, the identifier starts at 00 and the sequence at 1.
- **Foreign-currency transactions: unchanged.** When the consideration is expressed in foreign currency, the invoice must show both amounts, the total amount and the applicable exchange rate. This is not a new requirement: it was already set out in article 13(14) of Ruling 71 and repeated in its articles 14, 16 and 32. What changes is that it now appears only once, applicable to every means of issuance.
- **The "(E)" marker: unchanged.** It must accompany the description or price of goods and services that are exempt, exonerated or not subject to VAT, separated by a blank space and in parentheses.

## Requirements by type of document

Title III organizes the requirements by document. Article 8 sets the base catalogue, and the following articles add what is specific to each means of issuance and each type of tax document, as summarized below.

### 1. Invoices (Arts. 8 to 11 and 37)

- **Common requirements.** 12 minimum items, whatever the means: the heading *"Factura"* (invoice); consecutive and unique numbering; control number; issuer's identification and RIF; date and time of issuance; purchaser's identification and RIF; description with quantity and amount; adjustments and discounts; taxable base per rate; VAT amount shown separately; total value; and foreign currency with its exchange rate (art. 8).
- **Additional · digital means.** Total control numbers assigned; transaction code; corporate name, RIF, number and date of the ruling authorizing the digital printer; and date of assignment of the control number (art. 9).
- **Additional · fiscal printers.** Fiscal logo followed by the registration number at the end of the invoice; generic identification when the equipment limits the description; and separation of the description from the price (art. 10).
- **Additional · contingency forms.** Pre-printed control number; total control numbers; the phrase *"sin derecho a crédito fiscal"* (without right to input VAT) on copies; details of the authorized printshop; and date of production (art. 11).
- **Additional · producers and distributors of alcohol and alcoholic beverages.** Use, number, type and capacity of containers; type and trade name of the beverages; origin, indicating whether domestic or imported and the manufacturer's name; actual volume in liters and actual strength, where applicable; volume of anhydrous alcohol and weight of the beverage in kg; driver's name and identity card number; vehicle type and license plate number; and date of issuance (art. 37).

### 2. Debit and credit notes (Arts. 12 and 13)

- **Means rule.** They must be issued through the same means used for the invoice that gave rise to the adjustment. Anyone who invoiced through a fiscal printer cannot issue the note digitally, and vice versa.
- **Content.** The same requirements as the adjusted invoice, with mandatory reference to the date, sequential number, control number and amount of the original invoice. The original and copies bear the heading *"Nota de Débito"* (Debit Note) or *"Nota de Crédito"* (Credit Note).
- **When they apply.** Sales or services that are cancelled in whole or in part, or that give rise to an adjustment for any reason, and for which invoices were issued.
- **Cross-reference.** They must meet the requirements of articles 8, 9, 10 and 11 according to the means, except for the heading *"Factura"* (Invoice).

### 3. Delivery orders or shipping guides (Arts. 14 to 16)

- **Purpose.** Only to support the transfer of movable goods that do not constitute sales. Where the VAT Law allows sales to be supported by these documents, the invoice must be issued within the same month, referring to the guide, except for invoices printed by fiscal printers.
- **Requirements.** The heading *"orden de entrega"* (delivery order) or *"guía de despacho"* (shipping guide); the requirements of article 8(2), (3), (4) and (6) and those of articles 9 and 11 according to the means; description of the goods with capacity, weight or volume, characteristics and reason for the transfer; and identification and RIF of the recipient or of the issuer itself.
- **Price.** In transfers that do not constitute sales it may be omitted, stating the reason: repair, transfer to own or third-party warehouses, distribution or other causes.

### 4. Certificates of exonerated output VAT (Art. 17)

- **When they apply.** Only when so provided in the relevant Exemption Decree. They may be produced on physical or electronic forms, always by authorized printshops.
- **Content.** 12 items: the heading *"Certificación de Débito Fiscal Exonerado"* (Certificate of Exonerated Output VAT); consecutive and unique numbering; pre-printed control number and total control numbers; issuer's identification, tax domicile and RIF; date of issuance; supplier's identification and RIF; numbers, dates and control numbers of the invoices and notes received; total VAT amount; details of the authorized printshop; date of production; and security features to verify the document's authenticity and integrity.

### 5. VAT and income tax withholding certificates (Art. 18)

- **Numbering.** Consecutive, 14 characters, in the format YYYYMMSSSSSSSS: four digits for the year, two for the month and eight for the sequence, which must restart if that number is exceeded.
- **Content.** Identification, RIF and tax domicile of the withholding agent, or of the printer when not issued by the agent itself; date of issuance and delivery; supplier's identification, RIF, tax domicile and e-mail address; control number and number of the invoice or debit note; tax period; total amount, taxable base, tax due and amount withheld; and details of the authorized digital printer, where applicable.
- **Gaming and gambling.** Add the RIF of the prize debtor when different from the agent, the certificate number (by printer when issued through a Fiscal Betting Printer), the winner's identity card and the gross and net prize amounts.

### Common provisions (Arts. 19 to 27)

Statement that the document is issued under Ruling 93; numbering by *"serie"* (series) where there is no centralized system; retention of cancelled originals until the tax obligation is time-barred; freedom of design within the required items; 30 calendar days to keep using forms with outdated data after a change of name, corporate name or domicile; destruction of unused documents with prior authorization from the Regional Internal Revenue Office; prohibition of erasures and corrections; and evidentiary value of certified copies when the original is lost for reasons not attributable to the recipient.

## Where the adaptation effort is concentrated

The regime is general, but it does not weigh equally on everyone. Title IV and a few scattered provisions concentrate the burden on specific cases:

- **Invoicing on behalf of third parties (arts. 32 and 33).** This is the most demanding case. The issuer must deliver to the third party a certified copy of the invoice within five days of the following month (an obligation already set out in art. 32 of Ruling 71) and, in addition, a monthly report within the first five business days of the following month, with consecutive numbering, control number, identification of the third party and the total amounts of VAT and of what was invoiced on its behalf. That report supports the third party's revenues or output VAT. The deadlines run in different units: calendar days for the certified copy, business days for the report.
- **Invoicing from several branches or areas (art. 20).** Absent a centralized invoicing system, documents must be issued with consecutive and unique numbering preceded by the word "serie" and by characters that identify and distinguish them from one another. The rule also extends to contingency forms.
- **Producers and distributors of alcohol (art. 37).** The only sector-specific restriction on the means that survives: they may only invoice by digital means.
- **Service stations (arts. 34 to 36).** They may continue to use a daily document equivalent to the invoice, but that authorization is transitional: it lasts only until they complete the technological adaptation that allows them to issue an invoice for each fuel sale.
- **Air passenger transport (art. 28).** The ticket, boarding pass, baggage tag and air waybill do not by themselves constitute documents equivalent to an invoice. Issuing a tax invoice is mandatory, with itinerary, method of payment and a breakdown of net fare, airport fees and taxes.
- **Banking is largely excluded.** Transactions governed by the Banking Sector Institutions Law are excluded by article 3(3), with a single exception: financial leasing, which must comply with Ruling 93.

## The transition: what to do before April 19, 2027

Ruling 93 will enter into force 180 calendar days after its publication (Eighth Final Provision), that is, on April 19, 2027. That window is the message for obligated parties:

- **Choose the means of issuance.** Those who were required to use a fiscal printer may now move to digital, and those who already invoiced digitally must confirm that their provider holds the certification required under Ruling 121. Those who currently invoice with free forms or pre-printed invoice books (*formatos*) as their ordinary means must move to one of the two authorized means, digital or fiscal printer: free forms are removed from the catalogue in article 4, and pre-printed forms are reserved for the contingencies in article 6. The move need not be to digital; it is enough that it be to one of those two.
- **Build the contingency stock.** Request the forms and their control numbering from an authorized printshop, and keep them at each place of business.
- **Design the regularization procedure.** The next-day report in the event of a contingency requires a person in charge, a channel and a record. It is best documented before it is needed.
- **Review invoice templates.** Time of issuance, exchange rate, formal-taxpayer phrase and numbering by series when invoicing from several areas or branches (art. 20).
- **Use up or cancel existing forms.** Free forms and pre-printed forms on hand may be used until exhausted or until the entry into force, whichever occurs first. Once that period expires, the remaining books must be cancelled and decommissioned (First Transitional Provision). The same provision only allows taxpayers to keep contingency forms produced in accordance with article 11 of Ruling 93. Those books therefore cannot serve as contingency stock: they do not meet the new requirements, including the statement that they are issued under Ruling 93 and the phrase *"sin derecho a crédito fiscal"* (without right to input VAT) on copies.
- **Report the change of means.** Anyone switching to a different means must notify the Regional Internal Revenue Office or Sector of its tax domicile.
- **Formal VAT taxpayers.** The requirements of articles 3 and 4 of Ruling 1,677 give way to those of article 8 of Ruling 93, and the phrase *"Contribuyentes Formales del IVA"* (Formal VAT Taxpayers) must be added.

## What happened to the 2024 digital invoicing rulings

On December 19, 2024, SENIAT published two complementary rulings on digital invoicing in the same Official Gazette. Ruling 93 treats them differently, and the difference matters when hiring a provider.

- **Ruling 102 is repealed.** It is expressly listed in the Second Repealing Provision. Its content is absorbed by Ruling 93, with a change in nature: what was an optional regime, reserved to those who obtained prior authorization and closed to those required to use fiscal printers exclusively, becomes one of the two ordinary means of issuance, freely chosen.
- **Ruling 121 remains in force.** Ruling 121, which governs the conditions and requirements for software providers used to issue invoices and other tax documents, does not appear in either repealing provision. It is the natural complement to article 4(1.2) of Ruling 93: it defines how a technology provider becomes authorized, through certification before the National Internal Revenue Office (*Intendencia Nacional de Tributos Internos*), with a joint technical assessment by the Audit and Information Technology Offices, a 15-business-day period to decide, the obligation to seek new certification for each version of the system, and express grounds for revocation.
- **A point to watch.** Article 41 of Ruling 93 refers the technical and functional specifications for authorization as a digital printer to a Technical Manual that SENIAT will publish through its electronic channels. The Second Repealing Provision also repeals *"all provisions of equal or lower rank that contradict or conflict"* with the new ruling. To the extent that Manual sets requirements different from those of article 3 of Ruling 121, the new regime will prevail. Before hiring or renewing a technology provider, it is advisable to wait for the Manual to be published and verify that the certification remains valid under it.

## What did not change

- Authorizations granted to printshops under Rulings 141 and 102 remain in force (Third Transitional Provision), even though both rulings are repealed.
- Non-compliance is penalized under the Organic Tax Code. Flexibility in choosing the means does not relax the formal duty or its consequences.

## The practical takeaway

Ruling 93 replaces a control model based on equipment (the fiscal printer as a sector-based obligation) or on physical media (free forms or pre-printed forms) with one based on data: what matters is that the information reaches SENIAT, and the taxpayer chooses the route. For most companies, this means less rigidity and a decision they did not have before.

But freedom comes with new and less visible obligations. Keeping contingency forms that may never be used, reporting the next day what was issued during a power failure, and showing the time of issuance (in HH.MM.SS format) on every invoice are formal duties whose breach is penalized under the Organic Tax Code. Six months seem like enough time. They are not if the decision is postponed.
