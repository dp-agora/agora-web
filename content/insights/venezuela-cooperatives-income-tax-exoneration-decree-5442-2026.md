*The Executive Branch has once again exempted cooperative associations from Venezuelan income tax. The new decree is in force for one year and applies to the fiscal year in progress on October 7, 2026. It is the fifth consecutive decree since 2022, and each one was published after the previous one had expired.*

Until November 2014, the Income Tax Law exempted cooperatives from that tax. The reform enacted that year (Decree No. 1,435, Official Gazette No. 6,152 Ext. of November 18, 2014) eliminated the exemption. Since then, cooperatives have been income tax ("IT") taxpayers, and their relief depends on the National Executive Power issuing an exoneration decree under article 195 of the IT Law and articles 73 to 76 of the Organic Tax Code ("Code").

The difference is not merely formal. Venezuelan law distinguishes between an exemption (*exención*) and an exoneration (*exoneración*). Both relieve the taxpayer from paying the tax. An exemption is granted by law and lasts as long as the law is not amended or repealed. An exoneration is granted by the Executive, for the term and under the conditions set in the relevant decree. For a cooperative, this means that its IT for each fiscal year depends on the date on which the decree is published in the Official Gazette, which the cooperative does not control.

Decree No. 5,442 (Official Gazette No. 43,475 of October 7, 2026) ("Decree 5,442") is the latest in this series. Its text is almost identical to that of its predecessors. Its publication date, however, has concrete consequences.

## What Decree 5,442 Exonerates

- **Covered taxpayers (art. 1).** Cooperative associations incorporated under the Special Law on Cooperative Associations. The decree does not distinguish by activity or by size.
- **Covered income (art. 1).** Net taxable income from Venezuelan sources. Foreign-source income is not exonerated and remains taxable.
- **Scope (arts. 3 and 5).** The decree relieves the cooperative from paying IT, but the cooperative keeps its formal obligations: it determines its income under the rules of the IT Law and files its annual return reporting both taxable and exonerated income.
- **Term (art. 8).** One year, renewable for an equal period. The exoneration applies to the fiscal year in progress when the decree enters into force, that is, on October 7, 2026.

## The Conditions: Where the Benefit Can Be Lost

- **RIF update (art. 2).** To enjoy the exoneration, the cooperative must update its Unique Tax Information Registry (*Registro Único de Información Fiscal*, "RIF"), for which it must submit a valid Certificate of Compliance issued by the [National Superintendency of Cooperatives (SUNACOOP)](https://www.comunas.gob.ve/sunacoop/). Without a valid certificate, there is no exoneration. The cooperative should check the certificate's expiration date before its fiscal year-end and before filing its return.
- **Mixed activities (art. 4).** If the cooperative earns both taxable and exonerated income, common costs and deductions are allocated proportionally. This requires accounting records that separate both types of income.
- **Losses (art. 6).** Losses from the exonerated activity cannot be offset, in any fiscal year, against income from taxable activities.
- **Loss of the benefit (art. 7).** The cooperative loses the exoneration if it fails to comply with the requirements and obligations set forth in Decree 5,442, the IT Law, its Regulations and other applicable rules.

## Recent Practice: One Decree a Year, Always Late

Decree 5,442 is the fifth in a series that began in 2022. All of them have the same one-year term, renewable. None was renewed: every year, the Executive issued a new decree, always after the previous one had expired.

The texts are practically identical. The differences are in the wording: the 2023 and 2026 decrees refer to the "fiscal year" in progress, in the singular, while the 2024 and 2025 decrees refer to the "fiscal years" in progress. The plural does not broaden the benefit. It refers to the different fiscal years of different cooperatives.

What matters is the trend. Each decree was published later than the previous one: June 2022, June 2023, August 2024, September 2025 and October 2026. Between one decree and the next, there were gaps of two weeks to a month and a half with no decree in force.

## What to Do

- **Confirm the cooperative's fiscal year-end.** Verify whether the fiscal year in progress on October 7, 2026 is covered.
- **Update the RIF and keep the Certificate of Compliance current.** Do so before the fiscal year-end and keep proof of the update. Verify that the certificate is still valid when filing the return.
- **Separate taxable and exonerated activities.** Identify foreign-source income and any income that is not exonerated, and document the method used to allocate common costs and deductions.
- **File on time and in full.** The annual return must include both taxable and exonerated income. Failing to file it, or filing an incomplete return, puts the benefit at risk (art. 7).
- **Monitor withholdings.** Decree 5,442 does not address withholding at source. IT withheld during the fiscal year must be credited in the annual return, and any excess may be recovered through the mechanisms provided in the Code.
- **Plan for 2027 conservatively.** Until the next decree is published, the cooperative should project its cash flow for fiscal year 2027 as if that year were taxable.

## The Practical Takeaway

The IT exoneration for cooperatives has become a recurring benefit, but not an automatic one. It depends on an annual decree that arrives later each year, and its text ties the benefit to the fiscal year in progress on the publication date.

The structural solution would be an exoneration for a longer term, or the timely renewal that the decree itself allows. Until that happens, each cooperative should review its fiscal year-end, keep its Certificate of Compliance and its RIF up to date, and document its position fiscal year by fiscal year.
