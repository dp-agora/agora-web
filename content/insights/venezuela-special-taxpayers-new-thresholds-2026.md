*SENIAT has published a new administrative ruling on special taxpayers that raises the qualification thresholds twentyfold, moves control of large taxpayers nationwide to Caracas, and eliminates qualification based on a relationship with another special taxpayer. It entered into force on November 1, 2026, but it does not automatically remove anyone from the regime.*

Being a special taxpayer (*sujeto pasivo especial*, "SPE") is not just an administrative label. SPEs are the large taxpayers. An SPE files and pays under its own calendar, acts as a VAT withholding agent, makes biweekly VAT and income tax advance payments, is subject to the high net worth tax, is excluded from the tax inflation adjustment, is exposed to increased penalties, is supervised by SENIAT's Special Taxpayers Divisions and, if it is a legal entity, is subject to the large financial transactions tax ("IGTF") on payments made from its bank accounts (see the previous article in this series, [Who Are Venezuela's Special Taxpayers, and What Are Their Obligations?](/insights/venezuela-special-taxpayers-seniat)). Each of these burdens carries a concrete financial and administrative cost.

Since 2023, SENIAT Administrative Ruling No. SNAT/2023/00005 (Official Gazette No. 42,588 of March 14, 2023) ("Ruling 5") set very low thresholds. A legal entity could be qualified as an SPE with annual gross revenues equal to 30,000 times the official exchange rate of the highest-value currency published by the Central Bank of Venezuela ("TCMMV"), or with a single month of sales above 2,500 times the TCMMV. Since the highest-value currency is currently the euro, that meant roughly EUR 30,000 a year or EUR 2,500 in a single month. A large share of the country's small formal businesses fit within that definition.

Those limits became outdated very quickly, and the consequence is plain to see: SENIAT has designated as SPEs individuals and legal entities that, given their size and volume of operations, should not be in that category. For individuals the threshold was even lower: 7,500 times the TCMMV per year (approx. EUR 7,500) or 625 times the TCMMV in a single month. Independent professionals, small shops and family businesses ended up bearing the withholdings and advance payments designed for large taxpayers and, in the case of legal entities, the IGTF.

SENIAT Administrative Ruling No. SNAT/2026/00099 (Official Gazette No. 43,485 of October 22, 2026) ("Ruling 99") repeals Ruling 5 and entered into force on November 1, 2026. It keeps the legal basis and much of the structure of its predecessor, but changes who is qualified, who controls them and how to exit the regime.

## The new thresholds: 20 times higher

- **A single scale.** Ruling 99 eliminates the distinction between individuals and legal entities. The same threshold applies to both.
- **Regional threshold (art. 2(a)).** Annual gross revenues equal to or above 600,000 times the TCMMV (approx. EUR 600,000) according to the last two annual income tax returns, or monthly sales or services above 50,000 times the TCMMV (approx. EUR 50,000) according to the last three monthly VAT returns.
- **Capital Region threshold (art. 3(a)).** Annual gross revenues equal to or above 3,000,000 times the TCMMV (approx. EUR 3,000,000), or monthly sales or services above 250,000 times the TCMMV (approx. EUR 250,000), with the same reference periods.
- **The measurement period also changes.** Previously, the last annual return or *any one* of the last six monthly returns was enough: a single good month sufficed. The rule now looks at the last two annual returns or the last three monthly returns.

The table summarizes the jump, expressed in multiples of the TCMMV:

| Case | Ruling 5 (2023) | Ruling 99 (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **Regional · annual revenues** | 7,500 (individuals) · 30,000 (legal entities) | 600,000 for all |
| **Regional · monthly sales** | 625 (individuals) · 2,500 (legal entities) | Over 50,000 for all |
| **Capital Region · annual revenues** | 15,000 (individuals) · 120,000 (legal entities) | 3,000,000 for all |
| **Capital Region · monthly sales** | 1,250 (individuals) · 10,000 (legal entities) | Over 250,000 for all |
| **Reference period** | Last annual return, or any of the last six monthly returns | Last two annual returns, or last three monthly returns |

The wording leaves two open questions. First, the rule does not clarify whether *each* of the returns in the period must exceed the threshold or whether it is enough for them to do so in the aggregate. The correct reading requires each return to exceed it, because that is consistent with article 8, which measures revocation by individual fiscal years and monthly periods. Second, the rule does not specify the date on which the TCMMV is taken to convert the Bolívar figures in each return. Both questions matter to anyone close to the limit.

The last paragraph of article 2 retains a relevant rule: to measure revenues, any estimate made by SENIAT in its verification and audit procedures, as well as information obtained from third parties, will prevail where applicable. That rule still refers to *"net income"*, even though that qualification criterion has disappeared from the text.

## Large taxpayers nationwide move to Caracas

This is the least visible structural change. Under Ruling 5, the Capital Region Special Taxpayers Office controlled two groups: large taxpayers *domiciled in the Capital Region* (art. 3) and, regardless of domicile, hydrocarbons, gas and mining taxpayers (art. 4).

Ruling 99 merges both groups in article 3, whose heading subjects all of its subsections to the Capital Region Special Taxpayers Internal Revenue Office *"regardless of the location of their tax domicile"*. A company in Valencia, Maracaibo or Puerto Ordaz that reaches 3,000,000 times the TCMMV may be qualified and controlled from Caracas.

- **Practical consequences.** Procedures relating to SPE status are handled before the Office to which the taxpayer is assigned (art. 6), and audits outside Caracas may be carried out by officials seconded from other Offices or from customs (art. 15).
- **How the transfer takes place.** The last paragraph of article 13 applies the change-of-domicile rules to taxpayers that, having been qualified by a Regional Office, are later qualified and notified by the Capital Region Office. Pending proceedings (verification, audit, summary proceedings, appeals, enforced collection, refund claims) remain, in principle, before the Office that initiated them.
- **Internal Revenue Sectors disappear** as bodies that qualify SPEs (art. 1). Only the Capital Region Office and the Regional Offices may qualify them.
- **An overlap to be clarified.** Public entities acting exclusively as withholding or collection agents appear in both article 2(b) and article 3(b). Ruling 5 distinguished them by domicile; Ruling 99 eliminated that criterion. In practice, the competent Office will be the one that issues the notification.

## Who is no longer covered

Ruling 99 eliminates all cases of qualification by relationship and one of the cases applicable to individuals:

1. **High-earning employees.** Individuals working exclusively as employees with net income of 7,500 or 15,000 times the TCMMV.
2. **Partners, directors, managers, administrators and representatives** of Capital Region SPEs. Ruling 5 qualified them regardless of their own income (art. 3(d)).
3. **Shareholders of mixed companies and holders of shares** of hydrocarbons operators (arts. 4(b) and 4(e) of Ruling 5).

The informative return that Ruling 5 required from those who took over oil fields in the transition from operating agreements to mixed companies (art. 23) also disappears. The exclusions in article 4 remain, with a single adjustment: the exclusion for new registered ventures increases from two to three years from their registration in the National Entrepreneurship Registry.

## A subsection worth reading carefully: suppliers

Article 3(e) allows the Capital Region Office to qualify as SPEs *"the main suppliers, contractors and service providers of the persons mentioned in subsections a), b) and c) of this article"*.

Under Ruling 5, the equivalent rule (art. 4(d)) referred to hydrocarbons taxpayers. With the new numbering, subsections a), b) and c) of article 3 are now large taxpayers, public entities and hydrocarbons operators. A literal reading therefore reaches the main suppliers of large companies and of the State, with no revenue threshold and regardless of domicile.

Everything suggests this is an unintended effect of the renumbering: the operators under the former agreements, who remain in subsection d), were left out of the cross-reference. But the published text is what governs, and qualification is discretionary ("may"). Significant suppliers to the public sector or to large taxpayers should factor in that possibility. If a qualification arrives, it is an administrative act of particular effect that may be challenged on grounds of lack of proportionality and the purpose of the rule.

## Duties: everything electronic

- **Electronic filing and payment (art. 5).** Ruling 5 allowed payment at any National Funds Collection Office. Ruling 99 requires taxes and related charges to be filed and paid electronically, on the dates of the calendar SENIAT publishes each year.
- **No calendar exception for hydrocarbons.** Ruling 5 excluded hydrocarbons operators' income tax returns from the calendar (art. 7). That exception does not appear in Ruling 99; the annual calendar will govern their dates.
- **Procedures before the assigned Office (art. 6).** The rule no longer mentions filing appeals at the notified address or the electronic tax domicile. Appeals are governed by the Organic Tax Code.

## Loss and revocation: easier to exit, but not automatic

- **A merger causes loss of status (art. 7).** Previously, a legal entity lost it upon cessation of operations and liquidation. Now it also loses it upon a merger. The merger must be notified to the Offices of the merging entities within five business days, and the Office of the surviving entity must qualify it within three business days of notification (art. 10).
- **Revocation without Intendencia authorization (art. 8).** Ruling 5 required prior authorization from the National Internal Revenue Office (*Intendencia Nacional de Tributos Internos*). Ruling 99 grants the power to the Tax Administration without that intermediate step.
- **A new monthly criterion.** In addition to the last two fiscal years with revenues below the minimum, revocation is available when sales or services were below the threshold in the last six consecutive monthly returns. It is also available in the exclusion cases of article 4(a), (b) and (c).
- **Requirements that disappear.** A certified copy of the registered merger agreement, the filing of the death certificate by the heirs, and the reference to internal qualification manuals are no longer required.

## The key point: no one exits the regime on their own

Article 16 provides that taxpayers qualified and notified as SPEs before Ruling 99 entered into force do not lose that status. The consequence is direct: a company with revenues of EUR 200,000 a year, qualified under Ruling 5, is still an SPE after November 1, 2026, even if it does not reach even one third of the new threshold.

To exit, it must request revocation (arts. 6 and 8). In our view, the *"minimum established for its qualification"* referred to in article 8 is that of the rule in force, that is, Ruling 99. It would make no sense to measure revocation against a repealed threshold. The same applies to those qualified by relationship (partners, directors, shareholders): their case has disappeared, and if their own revenues do not reach the new minimum, they have grounds to request it.

Revocation remains discretionary ("may"). It is therefore advisable to file the request with a complete record: the returns for the last two fiscal years and the last six monthly periods, their conversion to the TCMMV, and evidence that there are no pending adjustments that could alter the figures. SENIAT can reasonably be expected to verify before deciding.

## The old and new rules side by side

| Topic | Ruling 5 (2023), until October 31, 2026 | Ruling 99 (2026), from November 1, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| **Thresholds** | Different for individuals and legal entities. Regional minimum for legal entities: 30,000 times the TCMMV per year or 2,500 in a month. | Single thresholds for all. Regional minimum: 600,000 times the TCMMV per year or over 50,000 per month (art. 2(a)). |
| **Reference period** | Last annual return or any of the last six monthly returns. | Last two annual returns or last three monthly returns. |
| **Capital Region Office** | Large taxpayers domiciled in the Capital Region, plus hydrocarbons, gas and mining nationwide. | Large taxpayers, hydrocarbons, gas and mining, regardless of tax domicile (art. 3). |
| **Qualifying bodies** | Capital Region Office, Regional Offices and Internal Revenue Sectors. | Capital Region Office and Regional Offices (art. 1). |
| **Qualification by relationship** | Partners, directors and administrators of Capital Region SPEs; shareholders of mixed companies and holders of shares of hydrocarbons operators. | Eliminated. |
| **Employees** | Could be qualified with net income of 7,500 or 15,000 times the TCMMV. | Eliminated. |
| **Suppliers** | Main suppliers of hydrocarbons taxpayers. | Main suppliers of large taxpayers, public entities and hydrocarbons taxpayers (art. 3(e)). |
| **New ventures** | Excluded for two years. | Excluded for three years (art. 4(a)). |
| **Payments** | Electronic or at any National Funds Collection Office. | Electronic only (art. 5). |
| **Loss of status** | Death; cessation of operations and liquidation. | Death; cessation of operations and liquidation, or merger (art. 7). |
| **Revocation** | For annual revenues below the minimum for two fiscal years, with prior authorization from the Intendencia. | For annual revenues or six consecutive monthly returns below the minimum, and for supervening exclusion, without prior authorization (art. 8). |
| **Existing SPEs** | Retained their status, except those excluded under art. 5. | Retain their status without exception (art. 16). |

## What to do now that Ruling 99 is in force

- **Measure against the new thresholds.** Review the last two annual income tax returns and the last three VAT returns, converted at the TCMMV of the measurement date. If the result is close to the limit, document the exchange rate used.
- **Assess a revocation request.** Those who do not reach the new thresholds should quantify the savings: biweekly prepayments, IGTF on payments from their accounts, and the operational burden of withholding VAT. With that figure, prepare the record and file the request with the assigned Office from November 1, 2026.
- **Large taxpayers outside Caracas.** Prepare for a possible reassignment to the Capital Region Office. Until they receive the new notification, they must continue complying before the Office that qualified them (rule in art. 12, applicable by analogy).
- **Suppliers to the State and to large companies.** Anticipate a possible qualification and prepare the arguments to challenge it if it is not justified.
- **Corporate reorganizations.** A merger causes loss of status and requires notification within five business days. The merger timetable must be coordinated with the SPE's filing calendar.
- **Means of payment.** Verify that the treasury function can pay all taxes and related charges electronically on the calendar dates.

## What did not change

- Nationwide qualification of hydrocarbons, natural gas and mining taxpayers, and of their related operations (art. 3(c), (d), (f) and (g)).
- Who cannot be qualified as an SPE (art. 4). The four exclusions in Ruling 5 remain, with the adjustment already noted for new ventures: (a) new ventures registered with the governing body, for three years from their registration in the National Entrepreneurship Registry; (b) those engaged exclusively in primary production in the crop, poultry, livestock, forestry, fishing and aquaculture subsectors, understood as the mere production of fruits, products or goods obtained from nature, without processing or industrialization; (c) community organizations, organized communities and People's Power bodies constituted as communes under the Organic Law on Communes and registered in the Unique Tax Information Registry (RIF); and (d) taxpayers that started commercial operations less than one year earlier, duly evidenced, except for financial, insurance and reinsurance institutions and those carrying out works and construction projects. Three practical points. Exclusions (a), (b) and (c) are also grounds for revocation (art. 8): a current SPE that comes to fall within any of them may request revocation of its status. Exclusion (d), by contrast, is temporary and is not among the grounds for revocation: it serves to oppose a qualification notified during the first year of operations, not to exit the regime later. Finally, primary production must be exclusive: any processing or industrialization activity forfeits the exclusion.
- The rules on dissolution, bankruptcy and liquidation of autonomous institutes (arts. 9 and 11), and on change of domicile (arts. 12 and 13), with one adjustment: in enforced collection, the Office that initiated it handles the matter through the auction and then sends the file to the Office of the new domicile.
- Breaches of tax duties are penalized under the Organic Tax Code.

## The practical takeaway

Ruling 99 corrects an evident mismatch: with thresholds of roughly EUR 30,000 a year, the SPE regime had stopped identifying large taxpayers and reached small and medium-sized businesses that bore burdens designed for large ones. The new thresholds return the regime to its purpose, and centralization in Caracas confirms that SENIAT wants to focus its control on the most significant taxpayers.

But relief does not come on its own. Article 16 keeps all current SPEs in the regime, and revocation depends on a well-documented request and a discretionary decision by SENIAT. For many companies, the difference between remaining an SPE or not is measured in the cost of the IGTF and the advance payments. That difference justifies acting now.
