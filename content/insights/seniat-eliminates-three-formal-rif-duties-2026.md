Three. And your company may still be complying with all of them. On August 12, 2026, SENIAT — the Venezuelan tax administration — published Administrative Ruling SNAT/2026/00080 in Official Gazette No. 43,435, which amended the rules on the Unique Tax Information Registry ("RIF") in force since 2013.

The reform eliminated three formal duties that every registered taxpayer has been meeting for more than a decade. Fewer filings, less paper, and less exposure to penalties for formal breaches.

![Infographic: SENIAT eliminated three formal RIF duties — what disappears and what remains (Ruling SNAT/2026/00080)](/images/insights/seniat-rif-formal-duties-2026.html)

## Three formal duties that disappear

- The RIF registration certificate no longer expires. Until now it was valid for three years from issuance and had to be renewed within the 30 business days following expiration. The new Ruling eliminated the expiration date: the certificate is personal and non-transferable, and its validity is verified online through SENIAT's electronic channels.
- Taxpayers no longer have to produce the certificate for tax officials. The 2013 rule required them to show it whenever a SENIAT official asked for it. That duty disappeared, because the official can now check the registry electronically.
- SENIAT repealed the duty to display the RIF certificate in a visible place at offices, branches, or business premises (what is known in practice as the "fiscal billboard").

## What remains in force

- The RIF number is still mandatory on invoices and other documents issued by the taxpayer, on contracts, on filings addressed to government agencies, and in accounting books.
- It must also appear on product labels and packaging and in advertising. Here the reform modernized the language: where the old rule listed cinema, television, newspapers, magazines, and billboards, it now refers to mass media and digital communications. Social media advertising is expressly covered.
- Updating registry data remains mandatory whenever the registered information changes, within the following month. SENIAT may request the update at any time through its electronic channels.
- Tax administrations other than SENIAT (municipal or national) may still require the RIF certificate to be produced.

## The RIF moves fully online

- The reform replaced the reference to the "Tax Portal" with "SENIAT's electronic channels," defined as the official website or any other technological application the agency creates. The change gives legal support to new platforms and applications.
- Registration, amendment, update, and other filings are governed by the technical specifications published through those electronic channels.
- Registered taxpayers may print their digital certificate whenever they need it, provided no event has occurred that requires updating their data.

## What should your company do?

- Check whether your internal compliance calendar still schedules the three-year RIF renewal. If it does, remove it and free up that resource.
- Confirm that your registered data is current. That duty did not disappear, and it now carries the fine risk in this area.
- Before taking the fiscal billboard off the wall, confirm that no other rule (municipal, sector-specific, or licensing) requires you to display documents at your premises. The Ruling repealed the duty to display the RIF certificate, not the others.

## What next?

The reform did not reduce the tax burden: it reduced the compliance burden. The change looks minor, but in Venezuela formal duties are the most frequent source of penalties, and every duty that disappears closes off a line of exposure. Taking advantage of it calls for a review of internal compliance processes, not simply for taking a document off the fiscal billboard.
