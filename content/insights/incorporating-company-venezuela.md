## Basic Aspects to Consider Before Incorporating a Company in Venezuela

Incorporating a company in Venezuela may seem like a simple, mechanical task, but it requires strategic analysis.  Before contacting a lawyer, consider these nine points:

1. **Type of Company.**  Generally, investors prefer to incorporate a stock corporation (sociedad anónima), as it is typically the most widely used and accepted corporate form by merchants, financial institutions, and authorities; it also limits shareholder liability.  U.S. investors, however, often prefer to register a branch of a foreign company or use other corporate forms due to U.S. tax considerations.

2. **Corporate Purpose.**  Depending on the activity, legislation requires obtaining prior authorizations (e.g., hydrocarbons, health, and finance) and meeting additional requirements, such as minimum capital based on the corporate purpose.

3. **Share Rights.**  Maintaining an efficient company requires defining whether all shares will have the same privileges or if it is necessary to issue preferred shares to separate economic rights (dividends) from political rights (voting).

4. **Shareholders' Agreement.**  In some cases, such as joint ventures, it is advisable to enter into a shareholders' agreement, which is a private document separate from the articles of incorporation.  The shareholders' agreement serves to establish confidential and detailed rules regulating the internal relationship among the shareholders or partners, complementing the basic legal structure.

5. **Shareholder Dispute Resolution.**  It is recommended to include alternative dispute resolution mechanisms—such as negotiation, mediation, and/or arbitration—in the shareholders' agreement and/or the articles of incorporation.  Generally, these mechanisms are more efficient and faster than ordinary court jurisdiction.

6. **Capital Raising.**  Traditional incorporation through the registry is not the only way.  Through the stock market, it is possible to incorporate a company via promotion by conducting a public offering of shares.

7. **Corporate Governance.**  Company management is typically handled by a sole administrator or a board of directors.  A board of directors requires a balance between control and agility. Granting directors "individual signature" authority streamlines operations, while "joint signatures" ensure greater control.

8. **Foreign Shareholders.**  The Autonomous Service of Registries and Notaries (SAREN) impose a series of additional requirements for a foreign individual or entity to become a shareholder in a Venezuelan company.

9. **Tax Planning and Investment Protection.**  The tax residency of shareholders changes the fiscal landscape.  Furthermore, the domicile or place of incorporation of the shareholders may allow access to investment protection against expropriations, nationalizations, and other forms of mistreatment, as well as the free transfer of funds in cases of exchange controls or financial freezes (corralitos).  Therefore, Double Taxation Treaties and Investment Protection Treaties play a fundamental role in the efficiency and protection of the corporate structure.
